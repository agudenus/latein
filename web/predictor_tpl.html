How this works

Stage 1 — the group. Every kingdom sits in a Neighboring Kingdom Leaderboard group: a contiguous block of kingdom numbers, which is really a launch-date cohort. Opponents come from inside it. This stage is now taken straight from Century Games' own alignment announcements rather than inferred, so for the ranges above it is exact.

Stage 2 — the pairing. Inside the group, kingdom number carries almost no signal — measured at the 11th percentile against random pairing. What sorts it is Matchmaking Points: top-100 player power, shaded by battle record and activity. That is the one input still missing here.

Why groups drift. Groups merge with their neighbour on a 28-day cadence locked to the KvK cycle, so the walls move. A kingdom that has played many cycles carries opponents from several boundary eras at once. Measured leak rate against current groups runs 0.0% for kingdoms with ~4 cycles played, 5.2% at ~4.6, and 8.8% at ~9.9 — the leak is history, not a soft boundary.
